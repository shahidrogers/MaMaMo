How to read this

MaMaMo starts from a baseline of published data to Q2 2026, Budget 2026 and current market prices, then works out how far a set of measures moves the economy from it. Measures start in Q1 2027 and are per year relative to a no-new-policy 2027 (operating spending +4.5%, development spending RM84bn, Petronas dividend RM25bn).

  • Fuel. The unsubsidised RON95 price is set by Brent plus the refining margin, converted at the exchange rate (RM4.52/litre in early October). BUDI95 holders pay RM1.99 within their quota, so the gap is the subsidy bill.
  • Petroleum revenue. Income tax lags oil prices by about two quarters and is more sensitive than the oil price itself, because upstream profits move more than prices. Royalties move with oil. The dividend is set by the budget.
  • Multipliers. RM1 of extra government consumption adds about RM1 to GDP. Cash aid adds about 0.6 and development spending about 0.5, because much of it leaks into imports.
  • Limits. Coefficients are calibrated, not estimated. The no-new-measures baseline is MaMaMo's, not the Treasury's. On MaMaMo's oil path it gives a 2027 deficit near 3.0%, below the 3.3–3.6% that forecasters expect, mainly because it books more petroleum revenue. Treat the changes more seriously than the levels.
